---
title: Pain au Chocolat
intro: Crisp, buttery layers wrapped around two bars of half-melted chocolate. It takes some patience with temperature, but the first bite straight from the oven is worth it.
prepTime: about 1.5 hours
waitLabel: Chill and prove
waitTime: overnight + about 3–3.5 hours
cookLabel: Bake
cookTime: 20–22 min
totalTime: about 2 days (incl. overnight chilling)
servings: about 10–12 pastries
ingredients:
  - group: Dough
    items:
      - name: Bread flour
        amount: 250 g
      - name: Cake flour (or plain flour)
        amount: 250 g
      - name: Instant dried yeast
        amount: 8 g
      - name: Sugar
        amount: 50 g
      - name: Salt
        amount: 10 g
      - name: Unsalted butter (softened)
        amount: 50 g
      - name: Iced water
        amount: 140 ml
      - name: Cold milk
        amount: 140 ml
  - group: Butter for laminating
    items:
      - name: Unsalted butter (cultured butter with 82%+ butterfat is best)
        amount: 250 g
  - group: Filling and glaze
    items:
      - name: Bake-stable chocolate batons (or good dark chocolate cut into sticks)
        amount: about 20–24
      - name: "Egg wash: whole egg (beaten and strained)"
        amount: "1"
      - name: "Egg wash: milk"
        amount: 1 tbsp
steps:
  - title: Make the dough (day 1)
    text: Put the bread flour, cake flour, yeast, sugar and salt in a mixing bowl (keep the yeast and salt a little apart). Pour in the iced water and cold milk and mix on low speed into a dough, then add the 50 g softened butter. Keep kneading until the dough is smooth and springy (it does not need to stretch into a thin film).
  - title: Chill overnight
    text: Shape the dough into a ball, press it into a flat square, wrap it in cling wrap and put it in the fridge so it can relax and slowly prove.
    timer: Chill 8–12 hours
  - title: Prepare the butter (day 2)
    text: Put the 250 g butter on baking paper, beat it with a rolling pin and roll it into an even square of about 15 × 15 cm. Put it back in the fridge. When you wrap it in the dough, the butter should be cold but bendable — not so hard that it cracks, and not so soft that it melts.
  - title: Wrap in the butter
    text: Take the dough out of the fridge and roll it on a floured bench into a rectangle of about 15 × 30 cm. Put the butter square in the centre, fold both ends of the dough over it so the butter is fully covered, and pinch the seams closed.
  - title: First fold (book fold)
    text: Turn the dough 90 degrees and gently roll it out along the open side to about 20 × 60 cm. Fold both ends in to the 1/4 and 3/4 marks, then fold it in half like closing a book. Wrap and chill.
    timer: Chill 30 min
  - title: Second fold (letter fold)
    text: Take the dough out, turn it 90 degrees and roll it out again. This time divide it into thirds and fold it like a letter. Wrap and chill again. All the folds are now done and the dough has lovely layers inside.
    timer: Chill 30–45 min
  - title: Roll out and cut
    text: Roll the dough into a large sheet about 4–5 mm thick, 20 cm wide and 40–50 cm long. Trim the uneven edges, then cut it into rectangles about 8 cm wide and 20 cm long.
  - title: Roll in the chocolate
    text: Place one chocolate baton on the short edge of each rectangle and roll it over once. Add a second baton, then gently roll to the end. Place seam side down.
  - title: Final prove
    text: Space the pastries out on a baking tray lined with baking paper and lightly brush with a thin layer of egg wash. Prove somewhere around 26–28°C (never above 30°C, or the butter inside will melt out) until they are 1.5–2 times the size and the layers are clearly visible.
    timer: Prove 2–2.5 hours
  - title: Bake
    text: Preheat the oven to 200°C. Just before baking, brush on another thin layer of egg wash (avoid the cut sides so the layers can rise). Bake at 200°C for 10 minutes so the layers puff up, then lower the heat to 180°C and bake until deep golden. Move to a wire rack to cool and let the steam escape.
    timer: Bake 10 min at 200°C + 10–12 min at 180°C
tips:
  - Temperature is everything with these. If the dough shrinks back a lot while rolling, or the butter gets soft and sticky, put it straight back in the fridge for 15–20 minutes before carrying on.
  - Keep the dough and butter at the same firmness to get a nice honeycomb crumb.
  - Don't let the final prove go above 30°C — once the butter melts, the layers are gone.
