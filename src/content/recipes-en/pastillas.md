---
title: Filipino Pastillas de Leche (Milk Candies)
intro: A milk candy from the Philippines. Condensed and evaporated milk plus toasted flour — no oven needed, just one frying pan and about 20 minutes for rich, milky sweets.
cookLabel: Toast flour
cookTime: 5–8 min
totalTime: 20 min
servings: about 15–20 candies
ingredients:
  - group: Pastillas
    items:
      - name: Plain flour (must be toasted first to remove the raw taste)
        amount: 45 g (about 3 tablespoons)
      - name: Milk powder (optional; adds a milky taste — or use 20 g more flour)
        amount: 20 g
      - name: Condensed milk (the main source of sweetness and stickiness)
        amount: 150 ml
      - name: Evaporated milk (softens the texture so it doesn't stick to your teeth)
        amount: 50 ml
      - name: Unsalted butter (adds flavour and shine, stops sticking)
        amount: 15 g
  - group: To coat
    items:
      - name: Caster sugar
        amount: as needed
steps:
  - title: Toast the flour
    text: |-
      Put 45 g plain flour in a clean, dry frying pan over very low heat, and keep stirring with a wooden spoon or spatula.
      When it turns from pure white to a light creamy yellow and smells slightly nutty, turn off the heat and tip it out.
      If using milk powder, let the toasted flour cool a little, then mix in 20 g milk powder. Never toast the milk powder in the pan — it burns very easily.
    timer: Toast 5–8 min
  - title: Heat the milk mixture
    text: Wipe the pan clean and add 150 ml condensed milk, 50 ml evaporated milk and 15 g butter. Heat slowly over low heat, stirring, until the butter has melted and everything is smooth and thick (small bubbles at the edges are enough).
  - title: Bring it together
    text: |-
      Sift the flour and milk powder into the hot milk mixture (adding it in 2 batches makes it easier to mix).
      Keep stirring and pressing with a spatula until all the flour has absorbed the liquid.
      When it is very thick, forms a dough and no longer sticks to the pan, turn off the heat.
      Tip it onto a plate or baking paper and let it cool until warm but not hot to touch.
  - title: Shape and coat
    text: Rub a little soft butter on your hands so it doesn't stick. Take a small piece (about 10–15 g) and roll it in your palms into a small log (about the length of your little finger) or a ball. Roll it in a plate of caster sugar until lightly coated.
tips:
  - "Heat control: keep the heat low the whole time, both for the flour and the milk, or it can over-caramelise and taste bitter."
  - "Texture: for soft and melting candies, take it off the heat as soon as it forms a dough; for a chewier bite, keep stirring in the pan for 1–2 more minutes to dry it out."
storage:
  - Wrap each one in baking paper or colourful candy paper, twisting both ends like a sweet.
  - They keep for about 3–5 days in a cool place, or 2 weeks in the fridge.
