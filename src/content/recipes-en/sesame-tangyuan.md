---
title: Homemade Black Sesame Tangyuan
intro: Frozen black sesame filling wrapped in soft glutinous rice dough, so it flows out when you bite in. A small piece of cooked dough kneaded back in keeps the balls from splitting as they cook.
prepTime: about 1 hour
waitLabel: Freeze
waitTime: 20–30 min
cookLabel: Boil
cookTime: 1 min after floating
totalTime: about 1.5 hours
servings: 16–20 balls
ingredients:
  - group: Runny black sesame filling
    items:
      - name: Black sesame powder
        amount: 100 g
      - name: Icing sugar (to taste)
        amount: 50–60 g
      - name: Lard or unsalted butter (melted)
        amount: 50 g
  - group: Glutinous rice dough
    items:
      - name: Glutinous rice flour
        amount: 200 g
      - name: Warm water
        amount: about 150–160 ml
steps:
  - title: Mix the filling
    text: Mix the black sesame powder, icing sugar and melted lard (or butter) until smooth. It will be a slightly runny paste.
  - title: Freeze to set
    text: Put the filling in the freezer so the fat sets.
    timer: Freeze 20–30 min
  - title: Roll the filling balls
    text: Once it has firmed up a little, quickly roll it into small balls of about 8–10 g each. Line them up on a plate and freeze again until hard.
  - title: Start the dough
    text: Put the glutinous rice flour in a large bowl, add about 4/5 of the warm water and work it with your hand into crumbly pieces.
  - title: Make the cooked dough piece
    text: |-
      This is the key to tangyuan that don't split: pinch off a small piece of the mixture (about the size of your thumb, around 20 g), flatten it and boil it until it floats.
      Scoop it out, put it back into the rest of the mixture, add the remaining warm water as needed, and knead everything together. Stop when it is smooth, as soft as an earlobe and not sticky. Cover with a damp cloth or cling wrap so it doesn't dry out.
  - title: Divide and shape cups
    text: Divide the dough into pieces of about 15 g and roll each into a ball. Press your thumb into the centre of one, then pinch around the sides to make an even little cup (the rim can be slightly thinner).
  - title: Fill and seal
    text: |-
      Take the hard sesame filling out of the freezer and place one in each cup.
      Press the dough close around the filling, trapping as little air as possible.
      Hold it in the curve between your left thumb and finger, push the filling down with your right thumb, and slowly work the dough up around it.
      Pinch the top closed, nip off any extra dough, then roll gently between your palms until round. Dust with a little glutinous rice flour so they don't stick.
  - title: Cook the tangyuan
    text: Drop the tangyuan into rapidly boiling water, then turn down to medium-low (high heat can split them). Gently push them around with the back of a spoon now and then so they don't stick to the bottom. When they all float and puff up, cook for about 1 more minute, then scoop them out.
    timer: Cook 1 min after floating
tips:
  - Lard gives the most traditional flavour; butter gives a milky one. The secret to a runny filling is enough fat — about 1 part fat to 2 parts sesame powder.
  - The harder the filling is frozen, the easier it is to wrap without falling apart or sticking to your fingers.
  - Kneading the cooked piece back into the dough is what stops the tangyuan from splitting.
storage:
  - "Freezing: if you're not cooking them right away, line them up on a plate with baking paper and freeze. Once hard, seal them in a freezer bag."
  - Cook them straight from frozen — no need to thaw, just drop them into boiling water.
