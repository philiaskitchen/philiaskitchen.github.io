---
title: Sydney's Classic Strawberry Watermelon Cake
intro: In the style of Black Star Pastry. Two layers of almond dacquoise with rose-scented cream and a whole slice of fresh watermelon, topped with strawberries, pistachios and rose petals. Fresh and light, not too sweet.
prepTime: about 1 hour
waitLabel: Chill to set
waitTime: at least 4 hours (or overnight)
cookLabel: Bake
cookTime: 10–15 min
totalTime: about 6 hours (incl. chilling)
servings: 1 cake (square or rectangular cake ring)
ingredients:
  - group: Watermelon layer
    items:
      - name: Watermelon (cut into a flat slice about 1.5 cm thick, the same size as your cake ring)
        amount: 1 large piece
      - name: Rose water
        amount: 1 tablespoon
      - name: Sugar
        amount: 1 tablespoon
  - group: Almond dacquoise (2 layers)
    items:
      - name: Egg whites (room temperature)
        amount: "5"
      - name: Sugar
        amount: 110 g
      - name: Almond meal
        amount: 150 g
      - name: Icing sugar
        amount: 50 g
  - group: Rose cream
    items:
      - name: Thickened / heavy whipping cream
        amount: 500 ml
      - name: Sugar
        amount: 3 tablespoons
      - name: Rose water (adjust to how much rose flavour you like)
        amount: 1.5 tablespoons
  - group: Topping
    items:
      - name: Fresh strawberries (halved or sliced)
        amount: about 250 g (1 punnet)
      - name: Roasted shelled pistachios (roughly chopped)
        amount: 2–3 tablespoons
      - name: Edible dried rose petals
        amount: to taste
      - name: Clear glaze or strawberry jam (optional, to make the strawberries shine)
        amount: to taste
steps:
  - title: Macerate the watermelon
    text: Put the watermelon slice in a baking tray or deep dish. Sprinkle evenly with 1 tablespoon rose water and 1 tablespoon sugar, and let it sit so the watermelon releases extra water.
    timer: Rest 30 min
  - title: Dry the watermelon
    text: Before assembling, lay the watermelon slice on paper towel and pat the surface completely dry. This step is important — it stops the cake from going watery and soft.
  - title: Preheat and sift
    text: Preheat the oven to 200°C. Mix the almond meal and icing sugar and sift them together.
  - title: Whip the meringue
    text: Whip the egg whites with a mixer until foamy. Add the 110 g sugar in a few batches and whip to stiff peaks (the peaks stand straight up when you lift the whisk).
  - title: Fold in the almond mix
    text: Gently fold the sifted almond and icing sugar into the meringue in two batches, cutting through the mixture so it doesn't lose air.
  - title: Bake the dacquoise
    text: Spread the batter on a tray lined with baking paper (about 1 cm thick) and smooth the top. Bake until lightly golden and slightly springy to the touch. Let it cool, then cut two pieces the same size as your cake ring.
    timer: Bake 10–15 min
  - title: Whip the rose cream
    text: Put the cream and sugar in a mixing bowl and whip until you see trails (soft peaks). Add the rose water and keep whipping until firm (medium-stiff peaks). Be careful not to over-whip, or the cream will split.
  - title: Assemble the cake
    text: A square or rectangular cake ring (no base) works best — line the inside with clear acetate to make it easy to remove. Place the first dacquoise layer in the bottom and spread on about 1/3 of the rose cream. Lay the dried watermelon slice on top, then add another layer of cream to fill the gaps. Cover with the second dacquoise layer, spread the rest of the cream evenly on top and smooth it.
  - title: Add strawberries and chill
    text: Arrange the sliced strawberries evenly over the top. For shiny strawberries, brush on a thin layer of glaze. Refrigerate so the cake sets and the flavours come together.
    timer: Chill 4 hours
  - title: Unmould and decorate
    text: Remove the cake ring, sprinkle the top with the chopped pistachios and dried rose petals, then cut and serve.
tips:
  - Always macerate the watermelon and dry it completely — this is the key to a cake that doesn't go watery or soft.
  - Rose water is strong. Start with a little, taste, and add more slowly.
  - Chilling overnight gives a better flavour and cleaner slices.
